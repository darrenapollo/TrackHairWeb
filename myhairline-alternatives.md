It gives you an AI scan of your hair from photos, sells guides and a membership, and sells prescription hair loss treatments after a licensed provider reviews your details. Most people looking for an alternative want one of those three, not all of them, so this page starts with that.

## Trichometrics

Trichometrics is an iPhone app that scans your scalp and hairline and gives scores from 0 to 100 for density, thickness, scalp coverage and thinning. It also estimates a Norwood stage for men and a Ludwig stage for women, and draws density maps and a hairline trace. Past scans are kept, so you can compare photos side by side, see trends and export a PDF. It also has a treatment tracker with reminders and a community section.

Choose it if you want numbers and a stage estimate after every scan. Like every app on this page, it says it is not a medical device and its readings can't diagnose anything. If you're looking for a Trichometrics alternative because you'd rather not get a score, ScalpAnalysis AI and Track Hair both avoid one.

## ScalpAnalysis AI

ScalpAnalysis AI runs in the browser. You take four guided photos, and it gives you a hairline stage based on the Norwood scale, a density tier, a scalp health tier and suggested haircuts. Its maker says it reports "unclear" when a photo can't support a reading. Each scan is saved in a dated archive, and the paid plan adds same-angle comparison and trends. It doesn't log treatments.

Choose it if you want a quick web scan without installing an app.

## HairMetrix

HairMetrix is a clinical tool, not a consumer app. Canfield Scientific makes it for dermatologists and hair clinics, and the iPhone app works with Canfield's VEOS imaging hardware to analyse magnified trichoscopy images and compare visits side by side. You can't use it on your own, but if your clinic has it, ask whether they can repeat the measurement at each visit.

## Online clinics

If what you valued about MyHairline.ai was getting a prescription, the alternatives are telehealth services. Keeps and Numan focus on men's hair loss. Miiskin connects people with dermatologists online. Each runs its own questionnaire and clinician review, and each works only in certain countries. Whether a prescription suits you is a decision for that clinician.

Most of these services handle prescribing and refills, and leave the progress record to you. The guide to [online hair loss programs](/online-hair-loss-programs/) covers what they check after you sign up.

## Track Hair

Track Hair is our app, and it's built for keeping a record over months more than for a one-off scan. You add the treatments you already use or were prescribed, log them each day with reminders, and take a guided set of progress photos at three fixed angles. A follow-up set taken the same way later produces a side-by-side progress report. The app also measures readings such as hair density and scalp visibility from your photos and draws a density map. The analysis runs on your phone, and it gives no overall score and no Norwood stage.

The readings are measured from photos, so they move with lighting and camera angle. Treat them as a progress check, not a diagnosis.

Choose it if your question is whether a routine you're already following is making a visible difference. Track Hair is iPhone only and free to download.

## How to compare any hair scan app

Whichever app you pick, a reading only means something next to an earlier one taken the same way. Before you rely on one:

1. Check that it keeps every past scan or photo, with dates.
2. Check that you can take the same angles again, in the same light, next month.
3. Look at whether it explains its readings or only gives a number.
4. Read what it says about diagnosis. A trustworthy app says plainly that it can't diagnose.

UK dermatologists published a standard protocol for hair loss photography in 2025 because, as they put it, standardized protocols "are lacking". Their main point applies at home too: fixed views, taken the same way each time, are what make a comparison fair.
