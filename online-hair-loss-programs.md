Online hair loss programs have made it simple to see a clinician without an appointment. You fill in a questionnaire, a clinician reviews it, and if they prescribe something it arrives by post. What happens over the following months is less clear. Pattern hair loss treatments take many months to judge, and few services say how they will check whether yours is working.

This page explains the main types of service and what to ask before you sign up. It doesn't recommend any service or medication. Whether a treatment suits you is a decision for the clinician who reviews your case.

> We read each service's own website on November 24, 2026. Services, countries and terms change often, so check the current pages before you join.

## The three main types

### Telehealth subscriptions

Services such as Keeps and Numan in the UK focus on men's pattern hair loss. Keeps describes a short online assessment followed by a review of your plan with a licensed clinician, and Numan an online consultation with UK-based clinicians. Treatment is posted on a repeating schedule. Hims and Ro work in a similar way.

### Online dermatology

Services such as Miiskin connect you with dermatologists, usually through messages rather than a video call. They cover hair loss along with other skin and scalp conditions, which matters if you aren't sure your hair loss is the pattern kind.

### Scan-and-treat websites

Some sites, such as MyHairline.ai, pair an AI scan of your photos with guides and prescription products. The [MyHairline.ai alternatives](/myhairline-alternatives/) page compares that model with scan apps and trackers.

## What they check after you sign up

This is where services differ most, and where their websites say the least. From their own pages:

| Service | Photos | Follow-up described |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keeps | Before-and-after progress photos are required to claim the guarantee | Ongoing care, formula updates, virtual appointments |
| Numan | Money-back terms ask for photos on day one and every 30 days, kept on your own device | Medical team by phone and email, monthly refills |
| Miiskin | The app helps you take consistent scalp photos to share with your dermatologist | Secure messaging for follow-up care |

Where photos are asked for, it's often to settle a guarantee or refund claim, not to review your progress. None of the three describes a fixed schedule of progress reviews. That doesn't mean they don't happen, but it's worth asking.

## Questions to ask before you join

1. Who reviews my case, and are they a doctor, nurse prescriber or pharmacist?
2. Do you look at photos of my scalp, or only my questionnaire answers?
3. Will anyone review my progress at a set point, such as six or twelve months? What do they look at?
4. How do I report side effects, and how quickly does someone reply?
5. What happens to my photos and health data if I cancel?
6. Do you operate where I live, and are you regulated there?

The [dermatologist questions guide](/blog/questions-to-ask-dermatologist-about-hair-loss/) has more, and most of them work for an online consultation too.

## Why your own record matters

Pattern hair loss treatments are slow. MedlinePlus says finasteride may take at least 3 months to show any improvement, and that you should expect improvement during the first 12 months. The NHS points out that these treatments don't work for everyone and only work for as long as they're used. A review at six or twelve months is only as good as the evidence you bring to it.

That evidence is simple to keep:

- A baseline set of photos taken before or when you start, at fixed angles and in the same light.
- The same set again every month or so.
- The days you took or applied each treatment, and the days you missed.
- Notes on shedding, side effects and any change your clinician made, with dates.

Keep the record yourself rather than relying on the service. You can take it to a different clinician, and you still have it if you cancel. The [progress photos guide](/hair-loss-progress-photos/) covers the angles and lighting.
