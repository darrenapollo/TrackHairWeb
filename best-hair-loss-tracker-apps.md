## What "best" means for a hair loss tracker

There is no single best app for every goal. Some apps document a treatment routine, some help align progress photos, some emphasize instant AI analysis, and others connect users with clinical care. The useful question is not which app has the longest feature list. It is which app keeps the evidence you will need months from now.

We compared each app on five practical criteria:

1. Whether it preserves a useful record over time rather than only producing a one-off result.
2. Whether photos can be repeated consistently enough to compare.
3. Whether treatment schedules, changes, or adherence can be kept beside the photos.
4. Whether the app is clear about being a tracker, wellness tool, or clinical service.
5. Whether pricing and platform requirements are visible before you commit.

## Track Hair: best for following a treatment routine

Track Hair is built around the months-long reality of treatment. You can set up minoxidil, finasteride, spironolactone, microneedling, PRP, red light therapy, transplant recovery, or a custom plan; record what you actually completed; capture repeatable progress photos; and keep dose, shedding, side-effect, and regimen-change notes beside the timeline.

**Choose it when:** the question you need to answer is whether you followed a routine consistently and what changed across three- or six-month reviews.

**Keep in mind:** Track Hair is a journal and tracking tool. It does not diagnose the cause of hair loss or replace medical care.

## Hairline: best for a focused photo-and-stage record

Hairline combines a questionnaire-based Norwood-style reference with aligned monthly photos of the hairline, crown, and temples. Its App Store positioning emphasizes a simple, private progress record rather than an elaborate care platform.

**Choose it when:** aligned progress photos and a straightforward visible-stage reference are your main priorities.

**Keep in mind:** a stage reference describes visible pattern, not the cause of hair loss, and is not a medical diagnosis.

## Hair Snap: best for an AI hair and scalp snapshot

Hair Snap Health & Style Scan emphasizes AI ratings for characteristics such as density, texture, and volume, then connects those readings to care recommendations and progress views. It is closer to a hair-wellness scanner than a treatment adherence journal.

**Choose it when:** you want a quick AI-led hair and scalp overview with care recommendations.

**Keep in mind:** lighting, camera angle, styling, and hair condition can affect image-based readings. Use repeated photos and longer comparisons if your real goal is measuring change.

## Miiskin: best for clinician-led care

Miiskin is an online dermatology platform that includes hair loss among the conditions supported by its clinician network in eligible locations. Its primary value is access to professional assessment and prescription follow-up rather than standalone regimen tracking.

**Choose it when:** you want hair-loss concerns connected to a qualified clinician and the service is available where you live.

**Keep in mind:** availability, consultation costs, and treatment services vary by location.

## Which type should you choose?

- **You are following one or more treatments and need schedules, photos, and history together:** choose a treatment journal such as Track Hair.
- **Your priority is aligning the same scalp views every month:** choose a focused progress-photo tracker.
- **You mainly want an immediate hair or scalp reading:** choose an AI scan app and treat the result as informational.
- **Your loss is sudden, patchy, painful, inflamed, or otherwise concerning:** seek a qualified clinician first. Tracking can support that visit but should not delay it.

Whatever you choose, the useful part is the record you can still interpret months later: repeatable photo conditions, a stable routine, and honest notes about what changed.
