Minoxidil is slow, and most people start checking the mirror within days. This guide sets out roughly when change becomes visible, according to the official drug information, and which photos to take at each stage so you have something fair to compare when you get there. Everyone's timeline is different, so treat the months below as review points, not promises.

If you haven't set up your photo routine yet, [how to track minoxidil progress](/blog/how-to-track-minoxidil-progress/) covers the room, light and camera position. This page covers what to expect and when.

## What the official sources say

Two sources give the clearest timings. MedlinePlus says you must use minoxidil for at least 4 months, and possibly up to a year, before you see any effect. The US label for 5% topical solution says results may show at 2 months for some men, while others need at least 4 months, and that the amount of regrowth is different for each person.

The label also lists reasons to stop and ask a doctor, including chest pain, a rapid heartbeat, dizziness, swollen hands or feet, scalp irritation, and seeing no regrowth in 4 months. Oral minoxidil is a different medicine with its own risks, used only when a clinician prescribes it.

MedlinePlus adds that minoxidil doesn't cure baldness. Most new hair is lost within a few months of stopping, so the timeline only runs while you keep using it.

## Month by month

| When | What people often notice | What to photograph and note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before you start | Nothing yet. This is your baseline | A full set of front, temples, crown, part line and top-down, in fixed light |
| Weeks 1 to 8 | Possible extra shedding, scalp dryness or itching | Shedding and irritation notes, with dates. Photos are optional |
| Months 2 to 4 | Usually little visible change. Some see early fine hairs | A full monthly set. Don't judge it yet |
| Months 4 to 6 | The first fair comparison with your baseline | A full set, compared side by side with the baseline |
| Months 6 to 12 | A clearer picture of how much you've responded | Monthly sets, with a longer review at 12 months |
| Beyond 12 months | Any regrowth lasts only while you keep using it | Keep the monthly set going, and note any change to your routine |

## The first weeks: shedding

Some people shed more hair in the first weeks of using minoxidil. It's widely reported, but it doesn't happen to everyone, and it's hard to tell apart from ordinary day-to-day shedding without notes. Write down when it started, roughly how much you're seeing (in the shower drain, on your pillow, in your brush), and when it eases.

Heavy shedding, shedding that carries on for months, or shedding with a sore, red or itchy scalp is worth showing to a clinician. The guide to [whether your shedding is normal](/blog/is-hair-shedding-normal/) explains what to record.

## Months 2 to 4: keep going, keep photographing

This is when most people give up, because the mirror shows nothing new. It's also when the label says results may start for some people, so keep taking the monthly set even if you don't look at it closely. If early fine hairs appear along the hairline or at the crown, they often show best in a close photo taken in the same light as your baseline.

Note anything that could change the photos: a haircut, a new shampoo, a change of formulation, or days you missed.

## Months 4 to 6: your first real comparison

Put your 4 or 6 month set next to your baseline, angle by angle. Look at the same areas each time:

- How much scalp shows at the crown and along the part.
- The shape of the hairline and temples.
- Fine, short hairs that weren't there before.

If the two sets weren't taken in the same conditions, the comparison won't tell you much. A brighter room or wetter hair can look like a loss or a gain. That's why the baseline matters so much.

## Months 6 to 12: the longer review

By 12 months you have a series of monthly photos, and the trend across them tells you more than any single pair. If you're seeing a clinician, this is the record to take. If nothing has changed by now, the [hair treatment review checklist](/blog/hair-treatment-not-working/) helps you separate inconsistent use, photo conditions and a genuine lack of response before you talk to them.

## Why other people's progress pictures mislead

Search for minoxidil or Rogaine progress pictures and you'll find dramatic before and afters. They're hard to learn from. The before is often taken in harsh overhead light with wet hair, the after in soft daylight with dry, styled hair, and people tend to post their best results. Different hair types, starting points and products make it harder still.

Use them, if at all, to see what areas people photograph. The only before and after that answers your question is your own, taken the same way each time.
